## Goal

Choose how the solver's player policy scores positions so that a status or setup move is picked for the right reason, and specify the acceptance scenarios and the queue lines that implement the choice. This is the step that makes the D6 wide move pool (every verified status/power-1 move, `widePool` option, default off) worth turning on. It is the stated largest unknown of `.omc/plans/driver-d6-d5-audits-d4.md` (Risks, third bullet).

## Context (verified)

- The player policy is a depth-1 expectimax (`web/src/engine/sim/driver/expectimax.ts`): for each legal move and each voluntary switch it clones the session, plays the real turn with `stepTurn` against 6 sampled opponent actions from the ported trainer AI (`sampleOpponent`, :139; `meanOver`, :164) and scores the resulting position with `evaluateState` (:70). A switch must beat the best move by `switchMargin` 0.05 (:203). Defaults: depth 1, samples 6, innerSamples 2 (:35).
- Because the turn is really simulated, a status move DOES change the state after the turn (the foe gets `status1` sleep, a side gets `spikesAmount`, the user gains HP). Only the scoring ignores it: `positionScore` (:77) is `me.hp - them.hp + 0.3*(aliveDiff) + 0.04*(stageSum diff)`, where `sideScore` (:47-67) sums HP fractions over the whole party, counts living mons, and sums the active mon's stat stages 1..5 clamped to +-6. Nothing reads `status1`, `status2`, `substituteHp`, `SideState.statuses`/`timers`, `FieldState.weather`/`statuses`.
- Consequence: a move that heals (Recover) is scored through HP, so it is seen; Spore, Will-O-Wisp, Toxic, Leech Seed, hazards, screens, weather, terrain, Substitute, Taunt, Trick Room score as pure passes (the foe also gets a free attack that turn, so they score worse than an attack).
- State that exists to be read: `status1` (sleep bits 0x7, poison, burn, freeze, paralysis, toxic poison, frostbite, bleed: `web/src/engine/sim/constants.ts:149-170`) on the active battler and on party mons (`SimPartyMon.status1`, `state.ts:451`); `status2` volatiles incl. confusion, substitute, nightmare, cursed, wrapped, escape prevention (`constants.ts:177-204`); `substituteHp` (`state.ts:146`); per-side `reflect`, `lightscreen`/auroraVeil, `tailwind`, `spikesAmount`, `toxicSpikesAmount`, `stealthRockType`, sticky web (`state.ts:476-520`) with timers; field `weather`, `trickRoom`, `terrain` and timers (`state.ts:559-602`).
- Existing measurement (`docs/battle-sim/solver.md`, "How it plays and scores"): depth 1 won 19 of 24, depth 2 won 21, at about 4x the cost; the old "highest base power" policy won 2. The env settings of those runs are not recorded; the committed bench defaults to depth 2. `expectimax.bench.test.ts` (gated by `SIM_BENCH=1`, env `EX_DEPTH`, `EX_SAMPLES`, `EX_INNER`) runs 12 trainers x 2 seeds.
- `positionScore` is also used for `finalScore`, which `solver.ts:405` folds into fitness as `clamp(finalScore / 6, -1, 1)` summed as a tie-breaker. Changing its scale changes the tie-breaker.
- Other pieces of D6 that this depends on: `candidateMoves` options `{wide}` and `SearchOptions.widePool` (queue line 112), verified-effect gate (`verifiedMoveEffects.ts`).

## Candidates

A. Hand-weighted terms added to the leaf score (new function `statusScore` summed with `positionScore` inside `evaluateState` only; `positionScore` is left alone so `finalScore` and the fitness tie-breaker keep their scale).
   - Per-side terms (twins must not over-claim: Will-O-Wisp into a special attacker can still be correct because burn chip is 1/16 per turn, so only twins where the move is clearly useless are asserted): status1 on the active mon and on benched mons (value = expected HP-fraction and tempo lost, derived from the C: end-turn damage fractions in `battle_util.c`, paralysis speed and full-para chance, sleep duration distribution, burn attack halving, toxic counter), volatiles (confusion, substitute HP as a fraction of max HP, leech seed/cursed/nightmare drain, escape prevention), side hazards by layer count weighted by the number of living benched mons, screens/tailwind by remaining turns, weather/terrain/trick room weighted by whether the sides' types/abilities benefit (needs a small helper; start with a fixed per-condition value and a turn-count discount).
   - Cost: negligible (a pass over 12 mons).
   - Risk: weights are guesses unless derived; the policy can then over- or under-use a move class.
B. Short rollouts. After the simulated turn, play K more turns with the default heuristic policy (`runBattle`) and score the final `positionScore`. Sees every effect without hand weights.
   - Cost: K x (clone + turns) per (choice x sample); at K=4 the leaf cost rises by about 4x, similar to depth 2. Noise: rollouts use the default policy, which is weaker than the expectimax one (the expectimax policy won 19 of 24 against 2 for the old base-power policy, but no figure exists for `runBattle`'s default heuristic as a rollout policy), so a status move's value is judged under a policy that does not exploit it.
C. Depth 2 (`DEFAULT_EXPECTIMAX.depth = 2`). Exists, measured 4x cost. Sees one more turn of damage, so it sees "sleeping foe loses a turn" but not hazards over a long bench, screens that last 5 turns, or setup payoffs beyond 2 turns. Its cost multiplies with a wider move pool.

## Recommendation (to be confirmed by the reviewer and then the user)

A as the primary mechanism, with the weights read from the C and unit-tested one by one, plus depth 2 kept as an opt-in for confirmation runs. B is rejected for the solver loop (cost, and heuristic-policy bias) but kept as a validation tool: the scenario suite can compare A's choices against an exhaustive rollout to catch a badly set weight.

## Risks / open questions

- Weights remain a model: 24-battle bench noise is about +-2 wins, so the bench cannot prove a weight is right; the C-derived unit tests and the scenarios do.
- Terms that reward a status being set can make the policy re-apply or stack it when immune; the anti-spam twins guard that, but only for the listed cases.
- The AI samples (6 per choice) are drawn before the player's choice, so a term that depends on the opponent's response (a foe that will switch out of a sleep) is not modelled.
- Depth-2 plus the wide pool multiplies cost: pool size is not bounded by this plan.
- Not decided here: D3 chains, D1 doubles; evaluation in doubles.
